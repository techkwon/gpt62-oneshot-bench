A Field Guide to Club FaunaPlate I · Coding Club · Field entry

Coding club’s mascot is the Debug Duck, borrowed from a habit every programmer already has

Debug DuckAnas debuggus
Habitat
Computer lab, usually perched beside a monitor.
Diet
Bread crumbs, edge cases and the odd stray semicolon.
Behaviour
Listens without judgement while you explain your code line by line, until you spot the bug yourself.
Field marks
Round reading glasses, rubber-duck yellow, trails curly braces.
Why a duck
“Rubber-duck debugging” is a real technique, so the club already owns this joke.
CallIt works on my pond.

A Field Guide to Club FaunaPlate II · Robotics Club · Field entry

Robotics gets Servo the Crab, a mascot that comes with its own grippers

Servo the CrabCarcinus torquatus
Habitat
Workshop floor, mostly under the competition table.
Diet
Zip ties, AA batteries and spare M3 bolts.
Behaviour
Only walks sideways when the drivetrain needs recalibrating. Has never dropped a part.
Field marks
Steel eye-stalks, riveted shell, yellow status LED.
Why a crab
Claws are nature’s grippers, the exact problem the club solves every season.
CallWill work for torque.

A Field Guide to Club FaunaPlate III · Broadcasting Club · Field entry

Broadcasting gets Echo the Parrot, because repeating yourself on air is the whole job

Echo the ParrotAra repetita
Habitat
Studio booth, perched on the boom microphone.
Diet
Sunflower seeds and soundbites.
Behaviour
Counts down out loud before every segment and has never missed a cue.
Field marks
Studio headphones, red-and-blue plumage, yellow wing band.
Why a parrot
Parrots are famous for repeating what they hear. Broadcasters do it for a living.
CallRepeat after me: we’re live.

A Field Guide to Club FaunaPlate IV · Science Club · Field entry

Science gets Axl the Axolotl, a born experimenter that regrows whatever didn’t work

Axl the AxolotlAmbystoma inquisitivum
Habitat
Lab bench 3, safety goggles always on.
Diet
Data, preferably peer-reviewed.
Behaviour
When an experiment fails, regrows it and runs it again with one variable changed.
Field marks
Six feathery gills, lab goggles, a flask of something teal.
Why an axolotl
Real axolotls can regrow lost limbs, which is a good model for retrying experiments.
CallI axolotl questions.

A Field Guide to Club FaunaProduction notes

Production spec: fixed sizes, capped colour counts and PMS-matched palettes keep all 12 pieces consistent

FormatProposed dimensionsColour instructions
Enamel pin
 32 mm tall (1.25 in)1.5 mm thick, hard enamel, die-struck and polished. 2 posts with rubber clutches; club name back-stamped. Up to 6 enamel fills per pin, PMS-matched. Outlines are the plating metal: gold (Coding), silver nickel (Robotics), black nickel (Broadcasting), rose gold (Science). Min. line 0.3 mm; banner text ≥ 2 mm cap height.
T-shirt
 254 mm (10 in) wideFront-centre print, top edge 76 mm (3 in) below the collar seam. Same artwork for sizes S–XXL; 230 mm wide on youth sizes. Max. 4 spot colours, screen-printed; add a white underbase on dark garments (navy, black). Flat fills only, no gradients. Min. stroke 0.5 mm. Garments: navy, heather grey, black, natural.
Backpack patch
 76 mm (3 in) roundEmbroidered, 75% thread coverage on twill, merrowed edge. Iron-on backing that can also be sewn on, for backpacks. Max. 7 thread colours, matched to PMS. Twill and merrow colours per club (right). Lettering ≥ 5 mm tall; outlines stitched as 1 mm satin.
Artwork deliveryOutlined vector files (SVG/PDF) at 100% size, one file per format, spot colours named by PMS. Approve a digital proof plus a physical enamel and thread swatch card before the full run.
PMS codes are nearest matches. Confirm them against physical swatches with the vendor.
